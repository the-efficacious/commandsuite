# Spine

The spine is the team's durable record: what the world must become, who
owes what, and what was decided. It survives an agent's context; the
agent's memory of it does not.

It exists because context is lossy and everything built on top of that
loss inherits it. An agent that has been compacted, restarted, or simply
run long has no reliable memory of what it agreed to — so the spine is
built on one property: **the cheapest way to do an important thing is
the way that records it.**

## The contract

The unit of work is a **contract**. It states what must become true,
about a named piece of the world, with someone accountable for it:

| field | what it is |
|---|---|
| `subject` | the piece of the world this is about — registered, never guessed |
| `title` | what a reader sees first |
| `criteria` | the checkable statements that together mean "done". One verdict each |
| `assignee` | who is doing it |
| `verifier` | who judges the criteria. Optional — and its absence is stated, not hidden |
| `authority` | who rules when a question is above the assignee's pay grade. Optional |
| `constraints` | standing rules the work must respect ("do not fix this here") |

A contract is its founding event: the id of the `specification` that
created it. There is no second identifier to keep in step.

Authoring or amending one requires **`spine.author`**. Reading is
baseline — what binds you is not privileged information.

## Subjects and revisions

A **subject** is a typed region of the world: `repo`, `pr`, `file`,
`issue`, `setting`, `package` or `doc`. Subjects can contain each other
— a file sits inside a repo — and containment is declared at
registration, so a rule declared on the repo cannot be dodged by acting
one level down.

Subjects are **registered, never inferred**. An integration may register
what its own observations name (the webhook is the flash; the
registration is only its caption), but nothing parses a repo name out of
a sentence somebody typed.

A **revision** is an immutable observation point on a subject, and it
always records how it was obtained:

```
revision { subject, value: "12d7b54…", how: observed | asserted,
           source: integration:github | member:rune, at }
```

There is no field for a bare value. `verified at abc123` cannot be
written down without `observed at 03:19:02 from the GitHub review event`
riding along — so a value someone typed from memory and a value a
webhook reported can never read identically. A contract whose bound
revision is no longer the subject's latest observed revision renders
**stale**: a reported relation, never an edit.

## Criteria, attempts and verdicts

Work moves through the record rather than beside it:

- **`attempt_post`** binds a revision and says the work is ready to be
  judged. It is a readiness signal, not a claim of success.
- **`verdict_post`** judges **one criterion at one revision** — `met`,
  `unmet`, or `cannot_verify`. Partial progress is representable
  because verdicts are per-criterion; a single pass/fail on the whole
  contract was never able to say "three of five".
- A verdict cannot come from the assignee. Arrival is not declared from
  the traveller's own album.
- **`contract_complete`** completes by **citing the verdicts that cover
  it**. If a criterion is uncovered, the refusal says which, and why.

A contract with no verifier completes on its result alone — and says so.
That is the honest shape for work nobody was asked to check, and it is
different from work that was checked and passed.

## The counter, and refusals that re-brief you

Every state-changing write carries `expected_state_rev` — the contract's
counter as you last read it. If you are behind, **the refusal returns
the events you missed, in full.**

That refusal *is* your re-brief. It is not an error to retry past with a
bigger number; it is the record telling you what changed under you.
A cancellation that raced a verdict conflicts and is shown the verdict.

## Orient

**`orient`** is the recovery call. No arguments, never refused, cheap by
construction. Call it after a restart or a compaction, or any time you
are not certain what you are working on.

It returns the contracts binding you and how, their criteria with the
verdict on each, the state and the counter, the subject and the revision
each sits at, the rulings that bind them, the asks awaiting you, and a
cursor into everything else. Guessing costs more than the call.

Recovery is one code path for every entry point — a floor signal, a
declared compaction, a stale-write refusal, or an explicit call all
reach the same place, so what you read never depends on which door you
came through.

## Asks, rulings and the citation lock

Some choices belong to someone else. **`ask_author`** raises one and
names the authority; **`ruling_post`** answers it. An ask binds the
asker until it is answered — that is the point of raising it.

The **citation lock** is what makes authorization a lookup instead of a
memory: while an ask is open in its scope, an act that the ask covers
must cite a ruling that permits it. You cannot recall having been given
permission; you cite the row, or you do not act.

You can still move without waiting. **`proceed`** goes ahead without the
ruling — on the record, as a decision somebody made, rather than as a
silence that later reads as approval.

## Focus

The **focus set** is the team's shared boundary of what is lit for
travel *now*. Membership is an authored event with a reason, gated on
**`spine.focus`** — never a UI filter, and never derived from what looks
busy.

It changes what is *scheduled*, never what is *true*: a contract outside
the focus set stays in the record, in listings, and in its owner's
queue, but generates no ambient nudges. Anything addressed **to you** —
an ask naming you, a verdict on your contract, a contract ending under
you — reaches you whether it is in focus or not.

## Discussion stays cheap

The record is expensive to get wrong; the conversation must never be.
**`discuss`** is ungated and has the largest cap in the surface, because
a thread nobody can afford is a thread that moves to somewhere the
record cannot see.

When a post turns out to have been a decision, **`promote`** turns it
into the typed event it always was, citing the post it came from. The
origin is kept — the promotion does not launder a remark into a ruling,
it records that the remark *was* one.

## Nothing is ever removed

- **Corrections staple.** A correction stands *beside* what it corrects,
  which stays readable in full. Terminal events can be corrected too;
  that is the only way a finished record changes.
- **Amendments version** the contract, and carry a `disposition`:
  `correction` (the prior text was never validly binding) or
  `scope_change` (work already underway finishes under the prior text).
  The amender states which, because it cannot be inferred from the text.
- **Contamination is disclosed, not erased.** Removing text without
  saying what it said is refused.
- **Supersession** links a successor when the subject moves. A
  destination is never silently retargeted.

## Provenance

Every event records how the record obtained it:

- **`native`** — it happened here.
- **`legacy_projection`** — it was imported from the objectives
  subsystem the spine replaced.

A legacy projection **never acquires native status**, and the store
enforces that rather than promising it: `spine_events` refuses any
update to `provenance` and refuses deletion outright, so a row cannot be
relabelled — nor rewritten by deleting and re-inserting it. Correcting a
legacy fact is done the same way as any other: a new native event that
cites or staples to it.

Imported work carries no revision and no verdict, because the old system
recorded neither. That absence is the honest state, not a gap waiting to
be filled — see [`csuite-import-objectives`](/docs/reference/cli) for what
the import carries and what it deliberately refuses to invent.

## What the spine will not do

- **Never take the photos.** No generated contracts, no auto-verdicts,
  no inferred revisions. Constraints come from authors; no derivation
  produces them.
- **Never light the room.** No pretended globally-consistent view —
  watermarks and "as of" instants.
- **Never let a derived value render bare.** Every caption, or nothing.
- **Never spend a member's budget on ceremony.** No reminders on quiet
  states, no summaries that advance receipts, no status pushed at
  someone who did not ask.
