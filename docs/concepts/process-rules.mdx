# Process rules

A process rule is something the team has decided about **how it
works** — the merge model, what counts as verification, who ratifies
what. Objectives carry the contract for one piece of work. Process
rules carry the contract for all of it.

## The problem they solve

A rule announced in chat is not a rule anyone can be held to.

Broadcast is the first thing an agent's context loses to compaction,
and a member who joins later never saw it at all. So the durable
surface holds the stale version, the correction lives in a message,
and the gap between them is invisible to everyone except the person
who happened to read the thread.

Process rules close that the same way [objectives](/concepts/objectives)
closed it for contracts: the rule is **state**, not an event. What a
member receives is always what applies now.

## Injected, not fetched

Rules in force are served on `GET /briefing` and rendered by the runner
into the agent's **fixed context** — the standing instructions handed
to the agent at start. That placement is the whole mechanism:

- fixed context survives compaction; chat history does not.
- a member who never saw an announcement still has the current rule.
- an agent does not need to remember to check.

An agent therefore never calls a tool to find out what binds it. The
[`process_rules_list`](/reference/mcp-tools) tool exists for the
things injection deliberately leaves out — retired rules, and the
anchor and version needed to ask for history.

## Anchors: identity that survives amendment

Every rule has an immutable **anchor** — `merge-model`, not
`squash-merge`. Text moves; the anchor does not.

This is what makes a **reversal** distinguishable from a **rewording**.
A team whose merge model changes twice in one evening cannot tell those
apart from a prose diff, and the difference is precisely what matters:
one means "you were wrong about what we do", the other means "we said
it more clearly".

Pick an anchor that names the *subject* of the rule. An anchor naming
its current content becomes a lie the moment the rule reverses.

## Provenance is required, and is not a formality

Rules carry where they came from:

| provenance | rendered to every member as |
|---|---|
| `director` | *stated by X* |
| `lead_uncontested` | *proposed by X and not contested — weaker than adoption* |
| `unattributed` | *no attributable origin* |

"A director said this" and "the lead proposed it and nobody objected"
bind differently. Rendering them identically launders the second into
an authority nobody actually exercised — so the distinction is stored,
required, and shown.

## Disputed is a real status

A rule can be `in_force`, `retired`, or **`disputed`** — real, being
followed by someone, but recorded in a form its own author will not
stand behind, or contradicted by observed practice.

A store offering only *in force* or *absent* forces a false choice:
assert the rule as settled, or drop it and hide that people are
following it. Both are wrong. A disputed rule **is injected**, and is
injected with its dispute reason and an explicit
*do not treat as settled*.

## Amendment: one field, one answer

Amending a rule requires **`objectives.create`** — the same permission
that amends an objective's contract. Changing what binds the team is
one authority, not two.

The gate is the *permission*, not the role. A member the rule binds
hardest still cannot amend it without the leaf; a member holding the
leaf can, whatever their title.

The leaf is **named wrong for this use.** It says `create` and it
governs amendment of both contracts and process. A second leaf was
considered and rejected: two names for one authority lets them drift,
which is worse than one badly-named authority.

Each amendment records two orthogonal things:

**`disposition`** — the same field with the same meaning as an
objective contract amendment, so *does work started under the old rule
finish under it?* has one answer across contracts and process.

- `correction` — retroactive. The prior text was never validly binding.
- `scope_change` — forward-only. Work already underway finishes under
  the prior text.

**`changeKind`** — what actually happened to the rule: `reversal`,
`refinement`, or `wording`. Recording a reversal as a wording change
destroys the one signal the stable anchor exists to preserve.

No announcement is sent, and none is needed. The amendment takes effect
because the rule is injected.

## History: retrievable, not resident

Amending changes what is injected. It does **not** grow what is
injected.

The block carries current state only; every superseded value lives
behind `GET /process-rules/:anchor/history`. A rule amended fifty times
costs the same context as one never touched.

**There is no total ceiling, and saying "the block is bounded" without
that qualification is wrong.** The honest statement:

```
resident history growth per amendment   0
per-rule text ceiling                   4096 (+ bounded metadata)
number of rules                         unbounded
total injected process block            UNBOUNDED
```

What holds is the property that matters here — **amendment churn
contributes nothing resident.** What does not hold is any ceiling on
the block as a whole, because nothing caps the number of rules.

That residue is real and worth naming. `instructions` inherits an
8192-character cap sized for authored text; rules ride in their own
briefing field specifically so they are not subject to it — which also
removed the only thing that would ever have caught unbounded growth.
Every rule is resident in every member's context, every session.
Twenty rules at 500 characters is 10 KB per member and nothing
anywhere reports it. A future cap on composed output has to bound this
field too, or it will bound `instructions` and leave the rules outside
it — which is how this field came to be outside in the first place.

## What this does not do

Stated plainly, because the alternative is a reader assuming otherwise:

- **Rules arrive at the next runner start.** They are not pushed into a
  running session. A teammate mid-session has not seen a rule you
  adopted a minute ago.
- **There is no in-flight recovery.** The context watchdog that
  re-injects a dropped briefing block does not watch this one — it
  selects blocks by substring-matching the composed `instructions`
  string, and these rules are not in it. A rules block lost mid-session
  returns at the next start.
- **A runner older than the field drops it silently.** The member
  operates without the rules and is not told. So *"present in every
  member's injected context"* is bounded by *"every member on a runner
  new enough to read the field."*
